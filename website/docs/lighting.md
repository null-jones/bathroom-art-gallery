# Lighting

Light damage is cumulative and cannot be reversed: fading of dyes and pigments
and yellowing of paper and fibre depend on the total exposure, which is light
level multiplied by time. Halving the light level halves the damage over the
same period. These limits apply to the light falling on the work, measured
with a lux meter at its surface.

## Limits by light class

Each category in `collection/categories.yaml` has a light class.

| Light class | Maximum | Materials | Categories |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High | **50 lux** | Paper (prints, drawings), watercolour, photographs, textiles and dyed materials, natural history specimens | Prints, Photography, Textiles |
| Moderate | **200 lux** | Oil, tempera and acrylic paintings, wood, bone, undyed leather | Paintings |
| Low | Not a constraint; keep under 1,000 lux | Metal, stone, ceramics, glass | Numismatics |
| Assess | Decide per object | Minerals that fade or darken, mixed media | Fossils & geology, Other |

50 lux is the Canadian Conservation Institute's benchmark for light-sensitive
works. The CCI describes it as adequate for a young viewer looking at
light-coloured objects once their eyes have adjusted; dark surfaces, low
contrast and fine detail can each need up to three times as much light, and so
can older eyes. In a room this small, dim the
whole room rather than lighting one work brightly beside a dim one, so the eye
adapts.

## Ultraviolet

- Use LED lighting; it emits very little ultraviolet.
- Where daylight or other UV sources reach a work, keep UV below 75 µW/lm.
- Glaze sensitive works with UV-filtering glass or acrylic.

## Daylight

Both windows are at the north end of the room. Direct or reflected sunlight
can exceed the limits above many times over. Do not hang works in the high
light class where window light reaches them, and fit the windows with blinds
or UV film if works hang near them.

## Exposure time

Exhibition lighting should be on only while someone is in the gallery. Wire
the art lighting to the room's light switch or an occupancy sensor rather than
leaving it on. This keeps the annual exposure of even the most sensitive works
low.

## Label lighting

Light placards evenly at 100–300 lux, without shadows, consistent with the
light on the surrounding wall.

## Lighting system specification

For the gallery's own DIY spotlights:

- LED, warm white (2,700–3,000 K) to suit the oxblood walls and gilt frames.
- Colour rendering index (CRI) of 90 or higher, so colours read accurately.
- Dimmable, so each light can be set to its work's light class.
- Narrow-beam spotlights for individual works; check the light on the work
  with a lux meter after aiming. Phone light-meter apps are only approximate.

The colour temperature and CRI figures are this collection's own choices, not
conservation limits.
